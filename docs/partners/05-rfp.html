BYU AI Foundry · 2026–2027

Request for Proposals

BYU Marriott School of Business is seeking industry partners for the 2026 to 2027 AI Foundry program.

Sponsoring organizations bring a real, open-ended business problem. A faculty-guided team of BYU Marriott students spends the full academic year designing, prototyping, and testing an AI-enabled solution to it, and delivers a decision system the sponsor owns outright.

Submit a Project →

Program Overview

The AI Foundry is a year-long applied program running September 2, 2026 through April 14, 2027. It sits inside the Marriott School’s Strategic Management program and is cross-listed for MBA and senior undergraduate students (MBA 693R and STRAT 490R).

Where BYU’s engineering capstones build physical and embedded systems, the AI Foundry builds business decision systems: strategy frameworks, prioritization and scoring models, forecasting and predictive analytics, financial models, and the software that puts those in front of a decision maker.

About the Students

Students are MBA candidates and senior undergraduates in Strategic Management at the BYU Marriott School of Business. Most have completed coursework in product management and applied AI before joining a Foundry team, and many bring prior industry or internship experience in consulting, finance, product, and analytics.

Strategy and business

  • Corporate and business unit strategy
  • Business model design and evaluation
  • Portfolio management and resource allocation
  • Scenario planning and competitive intelligence
  • Market and industry analysis
  • Commercialization and go-to-market

Data science and AI

  • Machine learning and predictive analytics
  • Forecasting and time series modeling
  • Applied LLM and agent development
  • Python, SQL, and modern AI tooling
  • Explainable model design
  • Data mining and statistical analysis

Financial and economic modeling

  • Financial forecasting and revenue modeling
  • NPV, IRR, and payback analysis
  • Sensitivity analysis and Monte Carlo simulation
  • Unit economics and margin analysis
  • Capital allocation and cash flow modeling

Product and delivery

  • Product discovery and customer research
  • Requirements definition and prioritization
  • Rapid prototyping and AI-assisted software development
  • Dashboard and decision tool design
  • Executive communication and synthesis

The program is directed by Scott Murff, Associate Teaching Professor of Strategy, formerly a principal product manager and analytics consultant at McKinsey & Company.

Program Goals

Over the year, student teams will:

  1. Translate an ambiguous business problem into a decision that can be made with evidence
  2. Design a repeatable framework with explicit criteria, thresholds, and business rules
  3. Build and validate AI and analytical models against real data
  4. Quantify uncertainty and show how recommendations change under alternative assumptions
  5. Deliver explainable outputs that a decision maker can act on and defend
  6. Communicate findings at an executive standard

Collaboration Format

Component Details
Duration 8 months, 2 semesters, 32 weeks
Period September 2, 2026 – April 14, 2027
Team size 4–6 students, one dedicated team per project
Student effort 8–12 hours per week, per student
Sponsor commitment ~1 hour per week
Modality Remote, with optional on-site visits
Faculty oversight Faculty director reviews all milestones independently

Each team carries a client lead who owns the relationship, builders who do the work, and a QC lead who signs off on everything before it reaches the sponsor.

The engagement follows six phases: problem definition and scoping, framework design, model build and prototyping, validation and sensitivity testing, refinement and integration, and final delivery.

Deliverables

Project-dependent, but typically include:

  • Strategy assessment and validation report
  • Decision framework with explicit criteria, thresholds, and business rules
  • Scoring and prioritization model
  • Predictive or forecasting model with documented performance
  • Scenario and sensitivity analysis
  • Optimization model under real capital and capacity constraints
  • Executive decision dashboard or prototype application
  • Explainable model output showing which assumptions drive each recommendation
  • Executive summary and final presentation
  • Full repository and handoff documentation

Every deliverable carries an explainability requirement. If the team cannot walk the sponsor through why the model recommends what it recommends, it is not considered complete.

Ideal Project Types

Strong fits include:

  • Portfolio and resource allocation: which products, markets, or investments deserve scarce capital and management attention
  • AI-enabled decision support: turning a recurring judgment call into a repeatable, evidence-based framework
  • Forecasting and predictive analytics: demand, revenue, price, churn, risk
  • Market entry and commercialization: where to play and how to win in a new category
  • Build, buy, or partner evaluation: structured evaluation of strategic options
  • Business model evaluation: pricing, channel, and unit economics analysis
  • Product strategy and prototyping: defining and building toward a new product direction
  • Operational analytics: decision systems for recurring operating choices

The strongest projects share three traits: the answer is genuinely not known in advance, the decision recurs so a framework is worth more than a one-off analysis, and enough data exists for the team to work with.

Poor fits: work that is already fully specified and just needs hands, staff augmentation on an existing backlog, production software engineering, projects requiring systems access that cannot be provisioned, and safety-, clinical-, or compliance-critical determinations.

Partner Expectations

Sponsoring organizations agree to:

  • Define the challenge statement collaboratively with the team during the first six weeks
  • Assign a single point of contact with authority to answer scope questions
  • Hold a standing weekly call of 30 to 45 minutes
  • Provide data access early, and provision any necessary accounts
  • Answer decision requests raised in the weekly update within one week
  • Participate in four milestone reviews across the year
  • Complete a mid-year and an end-of-year team evaluation
  • Accept public recognition as a sponsor, unless anonymity is requested

Sponsorship

$22,000 per project. The fee covers faculty oversight and direction, student team time across two semesters, software and API costs, data and tooling access, program administration, and the final delivery showcase.

Intellectual property: sponsors retain full rights to all deliverables, documentation, code, models, and IP produced during the engagement.

Confidentiality: every student signs the sponsor’s NDA, or the Foundry’s standard mutual NDA. Client data is confined to the approved project repository and approved tools, and local copies are deleted at the end of the engagement.

Capacity

The Foundry runs a deliberately small number of engagements per year so each team can be staffed and supervised properly. Projects are accepted on a rolling basis until capacity is reached.

Submission Process

  1. Submit a project concept through the program page
  2. The program director reviews for fit and follows up within one week
  3. A 45-minute alignment call covers scope, timing, expectations, and administrative steps
  4. Scope is confirmed in writing along with sponsorship and IP terms
  5. Detailed scoping continues with the student team through Milestone 1 in October
